01  How it fits together

The mod is a health model underneath a set of traits. The systems run for every character: blood instead of health loss, wound infection as a process, bandages that wear out, a concussion that heals, a night scored by its light, a slow Vitality score from diet, exercise and sleep. The traits are the choices: conditions that read those systems and push on them. Medication is one shared list every condition reads, with levels, build-up, side effects and overdoses. Age is a fourth layer: a band every character carries that changes how the body, the mind and the metabolism behave, prices other traits, and offers traits only one age can take. Every effect that lasts has a moodle, and most systems have a sandbox switch.

Systems read each other. A fever from an infected wound brings on migraines, spoils sleep, raises blood sugar and irritates asthma. Blood loss slows recovery. A bad night makes a depressive episode likelier. The factors multiply, and nothing is hidden from the health panel for long.

02  Traits

Conditions you choose at creation, or in two cases earn in play. Filter by kind or search by any word in a card.

Multiple Sclerosis

-15

Heat is the enemy, and your energy is a daily budget of spoons.

  • A heat load follows warm air (24 to 34 C) and body temperature; builds over half an hour, fades in twenty minutes once cool, twice as fast wet. A drink straight from a tap or river takes some off; bottles do not.
  • Warm: tire sooner. Hot: stiff, sore, clumsy hands; swings can throw the weapon (5% to 15%); pain to about 60. Overheated: severe pain and a 5% chance a minute the hands give out.
  • Flares about once a month for three to six days: heat hits half as hard again, stiff legs, weak hands, exhaustion.
  • Flares come sooner with a fever (up to x3), stress (up to x2) and spoon debt.
  • Spoons: 12 a day by default (sandbox MS Spoons a Day) after a good night, fewer after a bad one, capped at eight in a flare. Everything spends them by effort; at none you hit the wall and borrow from tomorrow. Rest, naps and coffee give some back or hide it.
  • Pills: prednisone makes a flare pass three times as fast; baclofen halves the stiffness; amantadine adds two spoons a morning. Starts on baclofen and amantadine, built up, with a bottle of each.
  • -1 Fitness and Strength; not with Athletic or Strong. Moodles: MS Heat, MS Flare, Spoons.

A Really Bad Day

-8

A CDDA-style start: drunk, sick, an infected cut on the forearm, no clothes, the house on fire.

  • The cut is already infected and won't heal until treated; it spreads into the body in about a day and a half. Disinfectant pushes it back, antibiotics always do.
  • You start soaked through as well. Disinfectant and a bandage wait in a container 15 to 40 tiles away. No marker.
  • Not with Hemophilia or Straight Edge. Balance still open.

Arthritis

-10

Stiff joints, flares in the cold and damp, a grip that slips.

  • Slower to move and swing. The cold is read from the joints' own skin temperature, which clothing, wind and wet feed: gloves and a coat keep a flare off.
  • Painkillers halve a flare while they last; prednisone cuts it to about a third (x0.35). Neither touches the everyday stiffness. Starts with a bottle of painkillers.
  • A swing can slip (1% calm, more panicked, hurt, tired or flaring) and land at about a third of the damage (x0.35). Past half a flare one slip in three throws the weapon; a dosed full flare sits exactly at half and is safe.
  • Not in the 20s; flares sooner in the 40s and 50s (see the Age table). Moodle: Stiff Joints.

Diabetes Type 1

-10

A hidden blood sugar model read with a meter, no insulin of your own.

  • How sugar moves is in Dosing insulin, under Medication: in short, it climbs with food and with time, and insulin is the only treatment that brings it down. The Blood Sugar moodle says out of range, not which way; the meter and a strip say which.
  • Low: shaky, tired, anxious; a bad low puts you on the floor. Steady Hands stops working; seizures likelier. High: thirst, and infections take hold faster; over 350 for two hours, health drains.
  • Starts with three pens of 40 doses, a meter and strips (the meter and strips even with Starting Medication off). Inject about half an hour before a meal, and a dose every five hours holds the creep.
  • Both types: tipsy or drunker, the moodle and the symptom messages go quiet, so drink hides a low. A high drains health only to a 15% floor until 24 hours have been spent over 350; after that the floor is gone and it can kill. An inhaler puff raises sugar by about 15.
  • A dose covers about 12.5 g of carbohydrate. First Aid 3: a food's or sugary drink's tooltip says fast or slow sugar and a rough range of doses; 6: a closer range, and the meter says how much insulin is still working; 9, or the magazine Living With Type 1 at any level: the exact doses, a warning to eat it in parts if it would spike you past 350, and the meter says what gets you back to 110. The meter's tooltip keeps its last reading.

Diabetes Type 2

-5

The same sugar model with the body's own insulin, limited by weight and age.

  • More resistance in the 40s and 50s, a little less in the 20s (see the Age table). Regular exercise takes up to 0.25 off it.
  • Metformin builds up over two days and lowers resistance. Starts on it with a bottle, a meter and strips.
  • Drink, a long high and the inhaler work as the last line of the Type 1 card says.

Heart Condition

-12

Strain builds into chest pain; push through it and your heart gives out.

  • Strain builds from endurance spent, sprinting and running, the Endurance moodle, panic from half way up, a load over capacity and cold air; it drains at rest. Faster with age (see the Age table), a smoking habit, caffeine, a Run Down body, a stimulant overdose and every attack survived (+20% each). The Chest Pain moodle shows it from 0.4 and 0.75.
  • At full strain, chest pain for 15 to 30 minutes: a pain floor, recovery at a third, no running or sprinting, swings cost half as much again. Rest passes it twice as fast. Push on (spend at Endurance 2+, try to run, swing) and each minute risks a heart attack (8%, half on beta blockers): out 5 to 15 minutes, 15 health, endurance gone; a second within a day takes health to the floor.
  • A week to recover in three stages (Weak Heart moodle): recovery x0.5 / 0.65 / 0.8, effort x1.5 / 1.3 / 1.15, melee damage x0.5 / 0.65 / 0.8. Each attack makes strain build a fifth faster for good.
  • Beta blockers halve the build and the attack chance; a tenth off recovery while built up. Starts half built up with one bottle, plus nitroglycerin: a tablet ends chest pain in a minute and halves the strain, then a headache; two in an hour and you faint.

Brittle

-8

Solid hits can fracture a limb.

  • A weapon or zombie hit of 2 damage or more has a 20% chance to fracture one of six limbs. Likelier with age (see the Age table).
  • Falls and car crashes go by the concussion scale: nothing under its floor (a fence hop, a bump at 11 km/h), about 22% for a fence at 50 km/h, 40% for a crash certain to concuss.

Asthma

-10

Irritated airways that build through four tiers to an attack zombies can hear.

  • Irritants: cold air, corpses, exertion when endurance is low, sprinting or running at any endurance, panic, smoke, a fever. A dust mask halves the cold and the corpses; a gas mask or respirator blocks them entirely and cuts the exertion and sprinting build to a fifth.
  • Tier 2: recovery at half speed, and the odd quiet cough. Tier 3: none, and coughing. Tier 4, the attack: endurance drains to empty and health to a 15% floor, loud coughing, and an attack that empties you can black you out.
  • Eases over about four hours at rest, twice as fast asleep, once the attack has ended; during an attack it barely eases on its own. The rescue inhaler ends it at once (sandbox Inhaler Loot sets how often they turn up). Smoking makes it worse. Starts with one inhaler. Moodle: Airway Irritation.

Hemophilia

-10

Blood does not clot. Bleeds never stop on their own.

  • Uncovered, the bleeding clock is held where it is. A bandage runs it down, the clock is remembered under the dressing, and a change of bandage carries on from there. A clock run to nothing stays closed.
  • With Blood on: open bleeds lose half as much again, a bandage slows one to a quarter, a soaked bandage is never worse than anyone else's open wound. Stitches stop a deep wound; a scratch stops only under a bandage. Clotting powder only halves a bleed.
  • With Blood off: every open bleed costs 0.35 health a minute on top of vanilla's, with no floor. It will kill.
  • Not with Good Clotter or A Really Bad Day.

Major Depressive Disorder

-8

Episodes that hold mood down for days.

  • Pain and stress drag mood; a bad night makes an episode likelier. An episode holds a mood floor by its severity, with a refractory window after.
  • Lifts: drink briefly, cigarettes, comfort food, nicotine gum a little, exercise, time outdoors, a real antidepressant regimen (a pill a day, 14 days to build, discontinuation if you stop).
  • Moodles: Depression, Antidepressants.

Migraines

-10

Hours of pain and nausea from personal triggers.

  • Triggers: bad sleep, thirst, stress, hangovers, caffeine or nicotine withdrawal, a concussion, fever, heat, corpses, a storm on the way, bright daylight, sleeping in a lit room. Of the eight personal triggers, three count double for each character and the other five half; a strong one is worked out once it has brought on two attacks.
  • No attack within 24 hours of the last.
  • An aura, then hours of pain and nausea with blurred vision. Daylight adds the most pain and halves recovery; a dark room and sleep speed it. Sunglasses halve the light's share.
  • Painkillers barely touch it. Sumatriptan in the aura halves the attack; during one it ends within two hours, with a heavy-limbed day after.
  • Starts with a sumatriptan pack down to its last two tablets. Moodles: Migraine, Light Too Bright, Heavy-Limbed.

Epilepsy

-6

Seizures with an aura; anticonvulsants cut them to a tenth.

  • About one in eight days rested and well; likelier tired, in withdrawal, hungover, feverish, concussed, stressed, dehydrated, in a diabetic low or bright sun.
  • Five to ten minutes of aura, then out for 2 to 5 minutes, which can concuss, then an hour of headache. Asleep, a seizure wakes you.
  • Anticonvulsants every 12 hours build up over five days. Starts on them with a bottle. Moodle: Seizure.

Fear of Blood

-6

Vanilla reworked: slower first aid, and fainting at the sight of a wound.

  • Treating a wound takes a quarter longer. Stitching (25%), pulling a bullet (35%) or glass (20%), or putting a bandage, a poultice or clotting powder on a bleeding wound, or cleaning a burn (10% each) can make you faint for about eight seconds. So can losing blood fast.
  • Cannot perform first aid on others, as in vanilla.

Anaemic

-4

Needs iron; short of it you tire, chill and rebuild blood slowly.

  • Iron drains over five days; fresh meat, fish, greens, eggs and iron pills top it up. A new character starts at 0.6 with symptoms from 0.4, so they begin about a day in with nothing eaten.
  • Short: slower endurance recovery, tire sooner, catch cold easier. Red cells rebuild at half speed after blood loss, and rebuilding spends iron. Infections climb faster. Moodle: Low Iron.

Gluten Intolerance

-4

Wheat brings a gut flare.

  • Bread, pasta, baked goods, beer and dishes made with them bring cramps, nausea and low mood after a short onset; about ten hours to clear. The flare scales with the carbohydrate: a slice of bread is about two thirds of one and clears in under seven hours. Moodle: Gut Flare.

Smoker

-4

Vanilla reworked: cravings by habit, withdrawal, damaged lungs; quit or relapse.

  • Cravings build at half vanilla's pace for a typical smoker, faster the heavier the habit. Withdrawal brings irritability held at the Angry moodle (which now has effects), hunger, and lighter, worse sleep.
  • Damaged lungs recover endurance slower and cough. Anyone who smokes regularly gains the trait; three weeks without loses it, and a smoke after that is a coin flip to relapse. For 90 days after quitting, stress or drink can set off a craving on cue, fading over time. Nicotine gum eases quitting.
  • Smoking speeds caffeine clearance, lets infections climb faster and slows deep wounds. Moodle: Nicotine Craving.

Vegetarian

-4

Refuses meat, fish and anything cooked with them.

  • Judged by food type, by name and by a dish's ingredients. The eat action will not start.

Germaphobe

-4

Dirty skin and clothes build stress; clean is a relief.

  • The four worst body parts count, not the average. Getting clean is a real relief. Wounds are a fifth less likely to take an infection. Moodle: Filthy.

Alcoholic

-4

Withdrawal after a day dry; gained by drinking, lost by a sober month.

  • Craving, low mood and poor sleep, then pain, nausea and the shakes; heavy drinkers get hallucinations and seizures.
  • The habit is tolerance too: more drink to feel it, hangovers a little milder. After the sober month the first drink is a coin flip to relapse. Moodle: Alcohol Withdrawal.

Caffeine Dependent

-2

Half a day without coffee or tea brings a headache.

  • Coffee, tea and caffeine pills end a craving; a mug counts by what went into it. Cola and chocolate only put it off (six hours a can, three a bar). A week dry breaks the habit. Moodle: Caffeine Withdrawal.

Hallucinations

-2

Phantom zombies, sounds, whispers and panic.

  • Episodes roll every ten minutes, likelier with stress, tiredness, night, sleep debt, a concussion, withdrawal or a fever. Sounds are audio only. No moodle, on purpose.

Tinnitus

-2

Your own gunfire deafens you for a while.

  • Half a dozen pistol shots or three from a shotgun put Hard of Hearing on for half an hour or more. The ringing makes light sleep lighter. Not with Hard of Hearing or Deaf. Moodle: Tinnitus.

Lactose Intolerance

-1

Dairy brings a mild gut flare.

  • Cramps, queasiness and low mood, gone in about six hours. Moodle: Gut Flare, topping out at the second level.

Straight Edge

-1

Refuses alcohol and tobacco, and so their relief.

  • Not with Alcoholic, Smoker, Hollow Legs or A Really Bad Day.

Renaissance Faire Geek

+10

+1 Spear, Long Blade, Axe and Blacksmithing.

Resilient

+10

Vanilla reworked: less prone to disease, and a chance to beat the Knox virus.

  • A hidden 25% roll when infected (sandbox Resilient: Knox Survival Chance). The lucky get sick like anyone else, then the fever breaks 40 to 70% of the way through. Every infection rolls again. Re-costed from 4.

Grit

+8

Feels pain a third less: the moodle and everything pain drives.

  • Holds its own share of the body's pain reduction at 35% of the pain felt without it, so it stacks with drink relief. Painkillers work as vanilla.

Fast Recovery

+6

Fast Healer folded in; blood comes back faster.

  • After a bleed, blood volume and red cells return half as fast again. Not with Fast Healer or Slow Healer.

Deep Sleeper

+6

Wakeful folded in; light rarely wakes you.

  • Light costs half the rest, and the dark does more good.

Steady Hands

+5

Dexterous folded in; a surgeon's hands and a gun hand.

  • Splints and stitches go wrong half as often; fresh stitches tear half as often; stitching, pulling glass or a bullet and splinting take a quarter less time.
  • Reloading, racking, and drawing or holstering from the belt are a quarter faster. Not while the hands shake. Not with Dexterous or All Thumbs.

Iron Gut

+4

Vanilla reworked: less food illness, and Iron Stomach folded in.

  • Rotten and burnt food does half the harm; food sickness climbs half as fast. Re-costed from 2. A save with Iron Stomach gets Iron Gut.

Keen Cook

+4

Vanilla reworked: knife work comes with the kitchen.

  • Vanilla's baking and cooking recipes, +2 Cooking and +1 Butchering, plus +1 Short Blade. Re-costed from 3.

Good Clotter

+3

Bleeds run down twice as fast and lose less blood.

  • Not while glass or a bullet is still in. With Blood on, a bleed loses a quarter less. Not with Hemophilia.

Thick Skull

+2

Concussed half as often, a quarter less badly, heals half as fast again.

  • Bounces Back folded in: faints, knockouts and shock half as long, and the sumatriptan day-after too. Any age. A save with Bounces Back gets Thick Skull.

Outdoorsman

+2

Vanilla weather resistance; takes twice as long to burn.

Cat's Eyes

+1

Vanilla night vision re-costed from 3; light wakes you more easily.

Early Riser

+1

Starts rested; every night scores a little better. Not with Night Shift.

Gym Regular

+1

Every exercise starts with a Fitness Instructor's head start (65 regularity in the 20s).

Hollow Legs

+1

Drink hits a fifth less; hangovers milder (x0.6) and shorter (x0.7). Not with Straight Edge.

Meal Prepper

+1

Starts on a good diet; variety counts over five days instead of three.

Night Shift

+1

Between 6 AM and 8 PM, light wakes you a quarter as easily and costs a quarter of the rest. Not with Early Riser.

In Their 20s

+6

Young, and the body shows it; the brain is a sponge; the metabolism runs high.

  • Endurance back a quarter faster; Fitness and Strength train x1.5; wounds heal and stiffness fades x1.5; night wakes count less against the sleep score. No extra profession level.
  • Any skill under level 3 trains x1.2. Hunger x1.15; caffeine, drink, food sickness and daily medication clear x1.2.
  • A healthier heart and less Type 2 resistance; Gym Regular starts at 65. Not with Handy or Arthritis. Fit and Fast Learner a point cheaper. Every number is in the Age table.

In Their 30s

0

In Their 40s

-2

Years on the job, in a body a little slower.

  • Two levels in the main skill, one in each other skill the profession boosts. The trade trains x1.1.
  • Endurance x0.92, Fitness and Strength XP x0.9, healing x0.9, stiffness x0.85, night wakes count more against the sleep score. Hunger x0.95; everything clears x0.9, so a daily pill holds longer and so does a hangover.
  • Strong and Athletic +2 points, Stout and Fit +1. Arthritis flares sooner; Brittle, Heart Condition and Type 2 bite harder. Handy gets an extra level of Carpentry. Every number is in the Age table.

In Their 50s

A lifetime of knowing how, in a body that keeps the receipts.

  • Endurance x0.85, XP x0.8, healing x0.8, stiffness x0.7, wakes x1.3, red cells x0.65, concussions x0.6, hangovers x1.5. Hunger x0.9; everything clears x0.8.
  • Strong and Athletic +4, Stout and Fit +2, Fast Learner +1; Slow Learner gives one fewer, Hearty Appetite one more.
  • Arthritis flares sooner still; Brittle and Heart Condition x1.5; Type 2 resistance +0.2. Handy gets an extra level of Carpentry. Every number is in the Age table.

04  For everyone

These run for every character, trait or not. Most have a switch on the Vitality Project sandbox page.

Vitality

Diet, exercise and sleep roll into one slow score.

  • Fresh, varied food lifts it; junk and neglect drag it. Training, or a day of hard activity read from the body's metabolic rate, lifts it. The night's score feeds it.
  • Fit and Thriving: faster endurance recovery, mood and stress relief, a little health back, faster healing, cold and infection resistance. Thriving alone adds a kilo of carry weight and x1.5 Fitness and Strength experience.
  • Run Down and Sluggish: slower endurance recovery and healing, a mood floor, colds and infections take hold easier. Health is never drained. Run Down alone loses the kilo.
  • A new character starts neutral and cannot fall below it for a day. Moodles: Vitality, Slept Badly.

Sleep and light

The light on your square while asleep sets how deep the sleep is.

  • Dark: tiredness drains faster, the night scores better. Lit: slower, worse, and anything brighter than reading light can wake you. Close the curtains.
  • Exhaustion, drink and sleeping pills sleep through it. Light wakes Restless Sleepers, Night Owls, Cat's Eyes, anyone within six hours of a coffee, the depressed or migrainous, the concussed (up to x2), the feverish, anyone in nicotine or alcohol withdrawal, and Tinnitus more easily. Desensitized characters have nightmares.
  • A sunburnt or hungover night scores worse.

Blood

Bleeding drains blood instead of health.

  • How fast: the game's bleeding clock, the part (neck x3; head, thigh and groin x1.5), and the dressing (a bandage a tenth, a shard through it, stitches stop it). Clotting powder packed into a bare wound from the health panel stops a scratch or cut, and holds a deep wound to a quarter for 12 hours (still stitch it); with glass or a bullet still in it only cuts the bleed to x0.6, and a hemophiliac's to half.
  • 15% lost: Pale. 30%: Light-headed, no sprinting. 40%: Shock, health draining, fainting. 45%: Bleeding Out. Half your blood is death.
  • Endurance recovers 25%, 50%, 80% and 90% slower by stage, and is capped at 30% in shock and 15% bleeding out.
  • Volume comes back in about a day if you drink; red cells take about a week. Moodle: Blood Loss.

Wound infection

Replaces vanilla's one-roll infection with a process.

  • Each hour a wound may take an infection: likelier deep, bitten, with a shard in, open, under a spent bandage or dirty clothes; far less once disinfected. Prone to Illness, Resilient, Diabetes, Vitality, iron and smoking count.
  • 8 to 16 hours unseen, then the wound's infection climbs about 2 a day and stops healing. Disinfectant or garlic clears a wound that has not shown yet, and pushes a local infection back 3 a day.
  • At 5 it spreads into the body: disinfectant and garlic stop working and only antibiotics help. The fever follows hours to a day or two later. Antibiotics: a level that halves every six hours, a dose every eight keeps it up, ten doses finish a course; stop early and it is a coin flip whether it comes back within a day. Past the fever, sepsis brings delirium and kills in a couple of days.
  • Zombie infection is untouched (Resilient aside). Moodle: Infection.

Wound care

Bandages wear out, deep wounds reopen, stitches tear, splints can be set badly.

  • A bandage lasts about a day, less wet or soaking blood; spent, it only halves a bleed and invites infection. Unstitched deep wounds heal at a third of the speed and hard use opens them.
  • Stitching is a First Aid roll (45% rough at level 0, 5% less a level, never under 3%; a suture needle or Steady Hands help). Rough stitches knit at half speed, tear twice as easily, ache and let infection in. Fresh stitches can tear: 2% a swing with that arm, 5% a minute sprinting on that leg, until they are sound.
  • Splinting is its own roll (50% bad at level 0, 6% less a level, never under 2%): set badly, the bone heals at half speed and aches. Walking on an unsplinted broken leg adds to the fracture time.

What First Aid tells you

The health panel is written for the examiner's level.

  • 0 to 2: what anyone can see. 3 to 5: the game's detail plus infected, a thinning dressing, a badly set bone, rough stitches. 6 to 8: how far an infection has got. 9 and 10: an infection that has not shown yet.

Concussion

A hard landing, a crash by speed, a car, or a hit to the head.

  • Dazed: headache, drowsy. Concussed: sick, daylight hurts, running or fighting can bring a dizzy fall, and a 25% chance of blacking out for 1 to 3 minutes. Badly concussed: out on the spot for 5 to 15 minutes. Rest heals it, sleep twice as fast.
  • A helmet makes one less likely (x0.4) and less bad (x0.6). A second knock before the first heals stacks on it.
  • A post-concussion headache often follows a day or two later, worse than the knock's own, lasting a day or two. Moodle: Concussion.

Passing out

Shock, a concussion and Fear of Blood can all put you on the floor.

  • You fall, sit, the screen goes black behind the menu, and you can do nothing until you come round: game minutes in real time, never under eight real seconds. Zombies can still reach you. In a car the engine cuts out.
  • A faint is shallow and a wound wakes you; a concussion knockout is not.

Sunburn

Bare skin in summer sun burns in about three hours.

  • Every uncovered part builds exposure in sunshine between 7 and 7, full from 20 C. A burnt part hurts for a day. Skin toughens with each burn; Outdoorsman takes twice as long.
  • Sun block, nearly as common as toothpaste, keeps the sun off for 8 hours a coat. Moodle: Sunburn.

Dehydration

Hours at Thirsty or worse build a load.

  • A headache, tiredness and slower endurance recovery; twice as fast Parched, three times Dying of Thirst. Drains in two hours once you drink. Moodle: Dehydration.

Anger

The vanilla Angry moodle now costs you, from Annoyed up.

  • Rough: melee hits wear the weapon more (never its last point); Furious swings cost endurance. Loud: from Angry up you curse out loud and zombies hear. Can't concentrate: reading x1.15 to x1.5. Sloppy: splints, stitches and vehicle parts go wrong more often.

Hangovers

Drink past a light buzz and one waits for you to sober up or wake.

  • At least six hours of headache, low mood, thirst and tiredness; two hours properly drunk is a full one, and even a small one is felt. Endurance up to 40% slower, worse in daylight, painkillers only dull it. A drink hides it and counts toward the next. Moodle: Hangover.

Drink relief

Vanilla's sip-of-whiskey miracle is undone.

  • Relief follows the Drunk moodle: pain reduction and panic by level. Being drunk lifts the mood too, faster the drunker: tipsy takes the edge off, blind drunk clears even a severe mood. The hangover is the price.

Moodles

Every effect that lasts has one. Needs Moodle Framework; everything else works without it.

05  Medication

One list of drugs that every trait reads. Each pill adds a level that a half-life drains. Daily drugs build up over days and fade slowly when missed; rescue drugs work while they are in the system; everything else keeps what it does and gains a level, a side-effect roll (3% a day by default) and a too-many effect. You are told when a protecting drug lapses. A character who starts with a condition starts on its drug, built up, and with Starting Medication on gets the bottle too.

Strips run out: test before bed, before a big fight, and when a symptom shows. The meter's tooltip keeps its last reading.

Age changes the half-life of daily and course drugs (x1.2 faster in the 20s, x0.9 in the 40s, x0.8 in the 50s). Cast Iron halves side effects and raises every overdose line by a pill. Daily bottles hold 30 and merge like vanilla's. Anticonvulsants, Metformin, Iron Pills, Diazepam, Nicotine Gum, the inhaler and Sumatriptan spawn partly used; Prednisone, Baclofen and Amantadine spawn full.

The Pill Caddy is a belt-worn weekly organiser that holds medication only: capacity 1, or 2 for the Organized. Uncommon in medicine cabinets, bedside drawers, handbags, pharmacy shelves and on the odd zombie, and a found one often has a bottle or two in it. A new character with a medical trait has a 1% chance to start with one. Clotting powder (5 uses) is a little rarer than bandages where they are kept, and turns up in first aid kits, medical bags, pet shops and salons. Living With Type 1 is among the health magazines: hospital magazine racks, medical offices, waiting rooms, pharmacies, bookstore and library medical shelves. Sun block is nearly as common as toothpaste; the prescription bottles turn up where vanilla's do (which ones come partly used is under Medication).

07  Moodles

Every lasting effect has one. The older systems feed their own; the rest come from one place. Four have a good side (Chest Pain, Seizure, Sunburn and Vitality at Fit or Thriving) and Antidepressants has only one, so five can show green; the icon going out is the reminder to take the next pill.
